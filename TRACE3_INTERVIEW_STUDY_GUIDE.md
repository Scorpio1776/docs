# 7. LIKELY INTERVIEW QUESTIONS & POWER ANSWERS

## Category 1: AI Strategy Experience

### Q: "Tell me about a time you developed an AI strategy for a client. What was your approach?"
**Framework for your answer:**
1. Start with business context (what was the client's challenge?)
2. Describe your discovery process (stakeholder interviews, data assessment, workshops)
3. Explain how you prioritized use cases (impact vs. feasibility matrix)
4. Highlight the roadmap you built (phased, with quick wins)
5. Share results (adoption rate, ROI, executive feedback)
6. Mirror Trace3's language: "data readiness," "crawl-walk-run," "people at the center"

### Q: "How do you conduct an AI use case discovery workshop?"
**Show your methodology:**
1. Pre-work: Stakeholder mapping, industry research, data landscape review
2. Workshop design: Cross-functional participants, structured ideation, voting/prioritization
3. Use case scoring: Business impact, data readiness, technical feasibility, organizational appetite
4. Output: Prioritized use case portfolio with quick wins and strategic bets
5. Follow-up: Roadmap with dependencies, resource requirements, success metrics

### Q: "How do you help a client who says 'we want to do AI' but doesn't know where to start?"
**Chris-aligned answer framework:**
1. Start with business problems, not technology solutions
2. Assess organizational maturity (people, data, process, technology)
3. Identify "low-hanging fruit" — processes with clear data, high manual effort, measurable outcomes
4. Recommend a phased approach with a 90-day quick win
5. Emphasize change management and executive sponsorship
6. Reference the Copilot path: "Many organizations can start tomorrow with tools they already own"

## Category 2: Technical Depth

### Q: "Explain RAG to a non-technical executive."
**Power answer:** *"Imagine you have the world's most knowledgeable consultant, but they only know what was published up through their training. RAG is like giving that consultant access to your company's internal documents in real-time — they can now answer questions using YOUR data, YOUR policies, YOUR customer history. It's the bridge between a general AI and one that actually understands your business."*

### Q: "What's your view on Agentic AI and where it's headed?"
**Reference Trace3's own thinking:**
- Agents are moving from single-task to multi-agent orchestration
- Key challenges: identity management for agents, governance, observability
- Trace3 identifies "Agent Orchestration" and "Agentic Platforms" as emerging 2026 trends
- The shift is from "scripting" AI to "platforming" it — enterprise controls, versioning, production monitoring
- Drop the line: *"The question isn't whether agents will transform enterprise operations — it's whether we'll have the governance infrastructure ready when they do."*

### Q: "How do you think about build vs. buy for AI solutions?"
**Framework:**
1. Core vs. context: Is this AI capability a competitive differentiator or operational hygiene?
2. Data sensitivity: How proprietary is the data? Can it leave the perimeter?
3. Total cost of ownership: Building sounds cheaper but maintaining is expensive
4. Time to value: Buy accelerates deployment; build enables customization
5. Talent availability: Do you have the ML engineers to build and maintain?
6. Reference MCP (Model Context Protocol) as an emerging standard for connecting AI to enterprise tools

### Q: "What's MCP and why does it matter?"
**Answer:** *"Model Context Protocol is essentially a standard way for AI models to connect to external tools and data sources. Think of it like USB for AI — a universal plug that lets models access your CRM, databases, documents, and APIs without custom integrations for each one. It matters because it dramatically reduces the integration tax on AI deployments and enables agent-based architectures where AI can actually take actions, not just answer questions. Trace3 is already tracking MCP Security as an emerging trend for 2026."*

## Category 3: Client Engagement & Sales

### Q: "How do you support a sales team during a client pursuit?"
**Answer framework:**
1. Join discovery calls to ask technical qualifying questions
2. Build custom demo scenarios relevant to the client's industry
3. Develop a preliminary use case assessment to show value before engagement
4. Lead a "proof of value" mini-workshop during the sales cycle
5. Create executive-ready materials that bridge technology and business outcomes
6. Quantify potential ROI with industry benchmarks

### Q: "Tell me about a time you influenced a C-suite decision around AI adoption."
**Structure your story:**
1. The executive's initial resistance or skepticism
2. How you reframed AI in business terms they cared about (revenue, cost, risk, speed)
3. The proof points you provided (benchmarks, case studies, POC results)
4. The outcome: budget approved, program launched, organizational shift
5. Use Chris's language: "trust, safety, and resilience" / "people at the center"
